NJS (2325): Extending water consulting into operations

Domestic water consulting and a large cash balance support the current value. The next step is evidence that long-term operations and digital tools produce recurring profit and value per share.

01

Summary

NJS is a specialist engineering consultant for municipal and public-sector water infrastructure. Planning, design and renewal are the core activities. The company is extending that base into facility operations, utility software, drone-enabled inspection and overseas consulting. FY12/25 revenue was ¥24.854bn and OP was ¥3.268bn. Domestic Operations produced 92% of revenue and all segment profit; Overseas Operations remained loss-making.

Aging water assets and a shortage of municipal engineers support medium-term demand.

FY12/26 H1 revenue reached ¥16.791bn and OP ¥4.966bn. H1 OP was already ¥1.366bn above the unchanged full-year forecast of ¥3.600bn. Management attributes the gap to large projects, early completion and a first-half-heavy delivery schedule. The 29.6% H1 margin therefore should not be used as a full-year run rate.

At the September 2 close of ¥4,710, market capitalization was ¥44.854bn. Cash and deposits less a conservative debt-and-lease proxy left net cash of ¥26.257bn, equal to 58.5% of market value. EV was ¥18.597bn, or 5.17× FY12/26 guided OP. The current value rests on Domestic consulting and cash. Long-duration operations and digital tools merit additional value when NJS discloses attributable revenue, margins, renewal and returns on invested capital.

02

Business

NJS reports two statutory segments. Domestic Operations plans, designs, reinforces and renews water and sewer assets and supports asset management. FY12/25 Domestic revenue was ¥22.910bn and segment profit ¥3.489bn. The Japan Sewage Works Agency accounted for ¥4.265bn, or 17.2% of group revenue. NJS has 63 domestic offices, 388 professional engineers and work records across 926 municipalities. That breadth supports local procurement and technical qualification, although it does not by itself establish exclusive contracts or pricing power.

Project progress and customer acceptance determine the timing of revenue and profit. The economic task is to convert national reach and qualified staff into repeat orders while absorbing higher personnel and subcontracting costs.

Overseas Operations has worked in more than 90 countries, but FY12/25 revenue of ¥1.945bn came with a ¥220m segment loss. H1 FY12/26 also recorded a ¥68m loss. Geographic reach and profitability should be judged separately.

Management also uses non-statutory business labels such as Consulting, Operation and Global. Operation extends design knowledge into long-term facility operation and maintenance; NJS currently operates at 26 sites. Its utility software is installed in more than 300 cities across 172 disclosed contracts. Inspection products include pipe drones, millimeter-wave water-level monitoring and underwater drones. They widen the municipal relationship, but recurring revenue, renewal and product margins are not disclosed.

03

Performance

Revenue rose from ¥19.232bn in FY12/22 to ¥24.854bn in FY12/25, an 8.9% three-year CAGR. OP fell to ¥1.619bn in FY12/23, then recovered to ¥2.993bn in FY12/24 and ¥3.268bn in FY12/25. OP margin moved from 10.1% to 7.3%, 13.2% and 13.1%, showing the effect of project mix and completion timing.

H1 FY12/26 orders were ¥14.080bn, up 15.3% YoY; revenue was ¥16.791bn, up 17.5% YoY; and OP was ¥4.966bn, up 27.0% YoY. Large domestic consulting projects drove the result. Full-year guidance remains ¥29.0bn orders, ¥28.0bn revenue, ¥3.6bn OP, ¥3.7bn ordinary profit and ¥2.45bn parent profit. The large gap between H1 profit and full-year guidance makes the full-year result and any FY12/27 reversal more informative than an annualized H1 figure.

H1 operating cash flow also rose to ¥9.398bn, helped by collections and contract liabilities. FY12/25 gives a better full-year reference: operating cash flow of ¥2.075bn less ¥0.661bn of cash purchases of tangible and intangible assets produced FCF of ¥1.414bn.

04

Outlook

NJS targets ¥40.0bn of revenue in 2030. Its FY12/26 business-line outlook is about ¥20.9bn for Consulting, ¥4.9bn for Operation and ¥2.2bn for Global, matching the ¥28.0bn group forecast. The company has not published 2030 revenue or margin targets for each business, so the path to ¥40.0bn cannot yet be quantified.

The 2024–2026 medium-term plan originally targeted ¥26.5bn of orders, ¥25.0bn of revenue and ¥2.5bn of OP in the final year. Current FY12/26 guidance is above those endpoints. The next question is which activity drives the next stage and how revenue converts into OP and FCF.

Long-duration operation is the most visible extension. The Toyohashi water-treatment project runs for about 30 years, and the Nagasaki/Nagayo project for about 22 years. The disclosed project-cost figures belong to the relevant consortia, however; they are not NJS revenue, backlog, economic interest or profit. Contract length alone does not establish attractive economics.

Software and inspection tools could improve productivity if each engineer can manage more assets. A Tokyo fixed-asset system contract is worth about ¥150m and targets roughly 1,300 hours of annual labor savings. Evidence of broader paid adoption, renewal and gross profit would show whether such projects are becoming a scalable business.

05

Financial position and shareholder returns

Cash and deposits were ¥26.309bn at the end of H1 FY12/26, and the equity ratio was 78.8%. Disclosed interest-bearing debt is minimal. Deducting all ¥52m of other non-current liabilities as a conservative debt-and-lease proxy still leaves net cash of ¥26.257bn.

How that cash is used will influence value per share. FY12/25 R&D expense was ¥928m, and the company indicates annual R&D of ¥1.5bn by 2030. NJS acquired CDC Aqua Service for ¥700m plus fees in 2025 and recognized about ¥503m of goodwill, amortized over ten years. The company has not separately reported returns from either investment.

The FY12/26 dividend forecast is ¥110 per share, up from ¥105 in FY12/25 and equal to a 2.34% yield at the reference price. Japan Hume held 3,420,000 shares, or 35.76%, at June 30, 2026. The allocation among dividends, R&D, acquisitions and any share repurchases—and the metric used to manage value per share for minority holders—therefore matters.

06

Share price and valuation

From September 2, 2024 to September 2, 2026, NJS shares rose 26.4% while TOPIX gained 50.3%, an underperformance of 23.8 percentage points. The period’s peak close was ¥6,680 on September 4, 2025, followed by a post-peak low of ¥4,295 on July 17, 2026. The reference close is 29.5% below the peak and 9.7% above that low. Sixty-day average traded value was about ¥143m per day.

At ¥4,710 and 9,523,112 shares excluding treasury and Board Benefit Trust shares, market capitalization is ¥44.854bn. Deducting conservative net cash of ¥26.257bn gives EV of ¥18.597bn. That is 5.17× FY12/26 guided OP of ¥3.600bn and 5.69× FY12/25 OP of ¥3.268bn. FY12/25 FCF yield is 3.15%; P/B using the H1 FY12/26 book value per share is about 1.46×; and the forecast dividend yield is 2.34%.

The cash-adjusted operating-profit multiple is low, while FCF yield is less striking. The distinction matters: cash already held and cash generated each year are different sources of value. Until long-term operations and digital economics become visible, the current EV multiple should not be treated as proof that future growth is already valuable.

07

What to watch

  1. Full-year normalization. How much of H1 OP of ¥4.966bn remains at year-end. Track H2 project completion, expenses, contract assets and liabilities, and operating cash flow together.
  2. NJS economics in long-term projects. NJS contract value, revenue timing, margin, equity and guarantee commitments, indexation and collection terms for Toyohashi and Nagasaki/Nagayo would allow a concrete valuation of Operation.
  3. Commercialization of software and inspection. Paid sites, renewal, recurring revenue, product gross margin and assets managed per engineer would distinguish scalable economics from tools that support individual consulting projects.
  4. Capital allocation and Overseas profitability. Investors need returns on R&D, CDC Aqua and future acquisitions, a path to lower Overseas losses, and a quantified use of surplus cash. Domestic consulting and financial capacity explain NJS’s current value. A higher assessment requires evidence that operations and digital expansion repeatedly increase earnings and cash per share.
