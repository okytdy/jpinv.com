NJS (2325 JP): Domestic consulting drives earnings; operations economics in focus

NJS's core business is planning and design for water and sewer infrastructure, with expansion into facility operations, utility systems and inspection. Earnings rose sharply in 1H FY12/26 as large Domestic projects progressed. The balance sheet carries Y26.257bn of net cash. The next issue is whether Operation and digital activities can develop into durable earnings streams.

01

Summary

NJS is an engineering consultant serving municipalities and public-sector bodies in water and sewer infrastructure. Its core work includes planning, design, seismic reinforcement, renewal and asset management. The company has expanded into operation and maintenance, utility systems, drone and other inspection technologies, and overseas consulting. FY12/25 sales were Y24.854bn and OP was Y3.268bn. Domestic Operations accounted for 92.2% of sales and generated the segment profit, while Overseas Operations remained loss-making.

Japan's aging water infrastructure is increasing renewal needs at a time when municipalities face shortages of technical staff. That creates scope for outsourcing beyond surveys and design into asset management, operation, maintenance and inspection. NJS has 63 domestic offices, 388 professional engineers and a work record with 926 municipalities. If it can sell operations, inspection and systems into existing municipal relationships, work per customer could expand.

1H FY12/26 (Jan–Jun) sales were Y16.791bn and OP was Y4.966bn. OP was already Y1.366bn above unchanged full-year guidance of Y3.600bn. Management attributes the gap to progress and completion of large projects being concentrated in 1H. We therefore do not regard the 29.6% 1H OPM as a normalized run rate. The immediate question is how much reversal appears in 2H and where full-year earnings ultimately settle.

At the September 2 close of Y4,710, market cap was Y44.854bn. Cash and deposits less a conservative debt-and-lease proxy leave net cash of Y26.257bn, equal to 58.5% of market cap. EV is Y18.597bn, or 5.17x FY12/26 guided OP. The multiple is low in part because of the large cash balance. A higher valuation would require evidence that investment in Operation and digital activities can generate sustained growth in earnings and free cash flow (FCF).

02

Business

NJS has two statutory reporting segments: Domestic Operations and Overseas Operations. Domestic Operations undertakes planning, design, seismic reinforcement, renewal and asset management for water and sewer facilities. FY12/25 Domestic sales were Y22.910bn and segment profit was Y3.489bn. Sales to the Japan Sewage Works Agency were Y4.265bn, or 17.2% of group sales. With public-sector work at the core, tender timing, budgets, project progress and customer acceptance can cause earnings to move between periods.

The company's 63 domestic offices, 388 professional engineers and work record with 926 municipalities form the operating base for bidding and execution across Japan. These figures do not by themselves demonstrate pricing power or high barriers to entry. Domestic profitability depends on allocating engineers efficiently to large projects while absorbing higher personnel and subcontracting costs.

Overseas Operations has project experience in more than 90 countries. FY12/25 sales were Y1.945bn. The segment loss was Y220mn in FY12/25 and Y68mn in 1H FY12/26. For now, we place greater weight on progress toward profitability than on expansion of overseas scale.

Management also presents business lines including Consulting, Operation and Global, which are separate from the statutory segments. Operation covers facility operation and maintenance and currently has 26 sites. Utility software has been installed in more than 300 cities, with 172 disclosed contracts. Inspection offerings include pipe drones, millimeter-wave manhole water-level monitoring and underwater drones. Adoption is visible, but NJS does not disclose the scale of recurring sales, renewal rates or product-level margins. We therefore need more evidence on economics before treating software and inspection as standalone growth businesses.

03

Performance

Sales rose from Y19.232bn in FY12/22 to Y24.854bn in FY12/25, an 8.9% three-year CAGR. OP fell to Y1.619bn in FY12/23 before recovering to Y2.993bn in FY12/24 and Y3.268bn in FY12/25. OPM was 10.1%, 7.3%, 13.2% and 13.1% over FY12/22–FY12/25, respectively, reflecting changes in project mix and completion timing.

1H FY12/26 orders were Y14.080bn, up 15.3% YoY; sales were Y16.791bn, up 17.5% YoY; and OP was Y4.966bn, up 27.0% YoY. Large Domestic consulting projects contributed, taking Domestic segment profit to Y5.035bn. Full-year guidance remains Y29.0bn of orders, Y28.0bn of sales, Y3.6bn of OP, Y3.7bn of RP and Y2.45bn of parent NP. It is unusual for 1H OP already to exceed the full-year plan, but management cites early completion of large projects. We will watch whether full-year earnings can still exceed guidance after any 2H reversal.

1H operating cash flow was Y9.398bn, but working-capital movements, including collections and contract liabilities, had a large effect. We do not regard this as a normalized full-year level. In FY12/25, operating cash flow of Y2.075bn less Y0.661bn of cash purchases of tangible and intangible assets produced FCF of Y1.414bn. Cash conversion from project progress is therefore another point to monitor.

04

Outlook

NJS targets Y40.0bn of sales in 2030. Its FY12/26 business-line outlook is about Y20.9bn for Consulting, Y4.9bn for Operation and Y2.2bn for Global, matching group sales guidance of Y28.0bn. The company has not disclosed 2030 sales or margins by business line, so the business mix and earnings path to Y40.0bn cannot yet be modeled in detail.

The 2024–2026 Medium-Term Management Plan (MTMP) originally targeted Y26.5bn of orders, Y25.0bn of sales, Y2.5bn of OP and a 10% OPM in FY12/26. Current guidance of Y29.0bn of orders, Y28.0bn of sales and Y3.6bn of OP is above those targets. The question is no longer whether the old plan can be achieved, but which businesses drive the next stage of growth and whether higher sales translate into higher OP and FCF.

Long-term operations are one possible growth area. The Toyohashi water-treatment redevelopment project runs for about 30 years, while the Nagasaki/Nagayo joint water-treatment project runs for about 22 years. Extending from design and renewal into operation and maintenance can lengthen the duration of awarded work. The disclosed project values, however, are consortium totals and do not represent NJS sales, ownership or profit. Assessing Operation requires disclosure of NJS-attributable contract value, margins and capital commitments.

Digital and inspection technologies are also relevant to municipal staffing shortages. A Tokyo Metropolitan Government fixed-asset management system contract is worth about Y150mn and targets roughly 1,300 hours of annual labor savings. If such projects lead to paid adoption and renewal at other municipalities, they could also improve consulting productivity. Current disclosure is not sufficient to judge recurrence or profitability, so we will watch earnings evidence alongside installation counts.

05

Financial position and shareholder returns

Cash and deposits were Y26.309bn at the end of 1H FY12/26 and the equity ratio was 78.8%. Interest-bearing debt is limited. We deduct Y52mn of other non-current liabilities as a conservative debt-and-lease proxy. Net cash is Y26.257bn. Financial capacity is ample. For valuation, however, the more important issue is how surplus capital is deployed and whether capital efficiency improves.

FY12/25 R&D expense was Y928mn. The company indicates annual R&D spending of Y1.5bn by 2030. NJS acquired CDC Aqua Service for Y700mn plus fees in 2025 and recognized about Y503mn of goodwill, amortized over 10 years. The direction toward greater investment in operations and digital technology is clear, but returns from R&D and acquisitions have not been disclosed in sufficient detail. We will focus on the contribution to earnings and FCF, not spending growth by itself.

The FY12/26 dividend forecast is Y110 per share, up from Y105 in FY12/25 and equal to a 2.34% yield at the reference price. Nippon Hume Corporation (5262 JP) held 3,420,000 shares, or 35.76%, at June 30, 2026, making it the largest shareholder. The balance among dividends, R&D, M&A and share repurchases will be important for medium-term capital efficiency and shareholder returns.

06

Share price and valuation

From September 2, 2024 to September 2, 2026, NJS shares rose 26.4% while TOPIX gained 50.3%, an underperformance of 23.8ppt. The period's peak close was Y6,680 on September 4, 2025, followed by a post-peak low of Y4,295 on July 17, 2026. The reference close of Y4,710 is 29.5% below the peak and 9.7% above that low. Average daily traded value over the latest 60 sessions was about Y143mn, so liquidity needs to be considered by institutional investors.

The reference price is Y4,710. Using 9,523,112 shares excluding treasury and Board Benefit Trust shares, market cap is Y44.854bn. Deducting conservative net cash of Y26.257bn gives EV of Y18.597bn. That is 5.17x FY12/26 guided OP of Y3.600bn and 5.69x FY12/25 OP of Y3.268bn. FY12/25 FCF yield is 3.15%; P/B using 1H FY12/26 BPS is about 1.46x; and the forecast dividend yield is 2.34%.

The low EV multiple is heavily influenced by net cash equal to 58.5% of market cap. FY12/25 FCF yield, by contrast, is 3.15%. We therefore think the operating cash generation of the business and the use of surplus capital should be assessed separately rather than relying on the EV multiple alone. If Domestic consulting remains profitable and investment in Operation and digital activities begins to lift FCF, there is scope for a valuation reappraisal. If returns on capital deployment remain unclear, the large cash balance alone may not lead to a higher share-price valuation.

07

What to watch

  1. FY12/26 full-year earnings. 1H OP of Y4.966bn already exceeds unchanged full-year guidance of Y3.600bn. 2H project completion and expenses should show whether 1H profitability mainly reflected early project completion or also an improvement in underlying earnings power.
  2. Economics of long-term operations. For Toyohashi and Nagasaki/Nagayo, disclosure of NJS-attributable contract value, sales recognition, margins, equity and guarantee commitments, indexation and collection terms would allow a more concrete assessment of Operation profitability.
  3. Monetization of software and inspection. Paid deployments, renewal rates, recurring sales, product GPM and assets managed per engineer would help show whether these activities remain tools that complement consulting or develop into new earnings streams.
  4. Capital allocation and Overseas profitability. We will watch returns on R&D, CDC Aqua and future M&A, together with progress in reducing Overseas losses and the use of surplus cash. Domestic consulting and financial capacity are strengths; the next stage of valuation will depend on returns from Operation and digital investment and on better capital efficiency.
