01 · Share-price regime

What moved the share price over the past two years?

stock price has approximately quadrupled from ¥897 in May 2024 to ¥3,485 in August 2025. In addition to the initial guarantee fee, renewal guarantee fees and monthly guarantee fees will accumulate from existing contracts, and it is assumed that profits will increase more than sales growth. Since then, it has lost about a third of its gains. The turning point was the FY9/25 full-year financial results and FY9/26 company plan announced in November 2025. Operating profit in FY9/25 increased by +81.5%, but the next year's plan was only +16.4%.

01 · The rally Nippon Insure is a Standard Market rent guarantee company, with guarantees accounting for about 94% of FY9/25 sales; listed peers include Casa (7196) and Zenhoren (5845). It guarantees rent for tenants unable or unwilling to provide a guarantor. Property managers submit applications through Cloud Insure, with underwriting using arrears data accumulated since October 2008 and JICC/LICC membership data. After paying landlords in arrears cases, it collects through an internal call center and seven regional locations. H1 FY9/26 collection was 99.1% and the incidence of recourse receivables 6.2%. Initial, renewal and monthly guarantee fees form three revenue streams; accumulated contracts build recurring renewal and monthly income. The shares reached a post-IPO low of ¥678 on August 5, 2024, traded around ¥800–900, then broke out after November 14 FY9/24 results showing sales up 12% and OP up 43%. They rose to ¥1,743 in spring 2025 and ¥3,485 on August 18, up 414% from the low. FY9/25 sales grew 16.0% to ¥3,737M and OP 81.5% to ¥759M as recurring fees lifted margins faster than SG&A. ROCE on average invested capital rose from 25.6% to 32.3%. About 8.8x next-twelve-month EV/EBIT at the high assumed this profit level would become the new steady state.

02 · The reversal The reversal began on November 14, 2025. FY9/25 OP was ¥759M (+81.5%), with a 20.3% consolidated margin and about 28% on a segment-weighted basis. FY9/26 guidance was ¥4,233M sales (+13.3%) and ¥883M OP (+16.4%). Slower OP growth made August’s valuation harder to justify. FY9/25’s recurring renewal and monthly fees had changed an earnings model centered on initial fees, but that step-up does not repeat at the same rate annually. FY9/26 also included estimated annual amortization of ¥40–60M for the ¥306M Cloud Insure upgrade through November 2027, and staffing investment in cities including Nagoya. H1 initial guarantee contracts fell 1.4% to 15,145; new inflows are needed to replenish recurring fees. The shares fell toward ¥2,200–2,400 around December’s AGM and temporarily stabilized there as investors priced in more normal profit growth against higher costs.

03 · Current position H1 FY9/26 results were released on May 14, 2026. Sales were ¥2,064M (+15.6%), OP ¥590M (+52.5%) and net profit ¥417M (+53.3%). The 28.6% OP margin was 7.7ppt above full-year guidance of 20.9%. H1 achieved 49% of the full-year sales plan and 67% of the OP plan. Incremental OP margin (change in OP divided by change in sales) was 0.73, slightly above 0.66 between FY9/24 and FY9/25. Credit quality also improved: the incidence of recourse receivables fell from 6.4% to 6.2%, and the collection rate rose from 98.8% to 99.1%. The company may be selecting contracts for profitability and collectability rather than pursuing volume indiscriminately. The equity ratio reached 51.4%, cash and deposits ¥2,760M and net cash ¥2,265M, or 33% of its ¥6,939M market capitalization. The FY9/26 dividend is planned to rise from ¥19 to ¥22, but the payout remains about 10%. No buyback has been authorized and management has made no comment about revising the dividend policy. Over the next four quarters, watch whether H1’s high incremental margin continues in H2, whether initial guarantee contracts recover through the Nagoya branch and new business partners, and whether November’s FY9/26 results bring a stronger capital policy from a business earning 32% ROCE with ¥2.26bn in net cash.

Issue 02 · Initial guarantee contract
Are initial guarantee contracts slowing down? If growth slows down, will future guarantee renewal premiums stop growing?
Bullish Bulls are looking at the number of initial guaranteed contracts in H1.15,145 (-1.4%)This is seen as a result of narrowing down credit rather than a lack of demand. During the same period, the incidence of receivables improved and the recovery rate rose to 99.1%. It appears that the company prioritized the possibility of collection and profitability rather than tracking the number of cases. The Nagoya branch, which opened in March 2024, is still in the process of being launched, and the same examination and collection platform can be used in adjacent areas such as nursing care expense guarantee and hospitalization expense guarantee. Bearish Bears believe that an influx of new contracts is essential for increasing renewal premiums. Regional concentration in Kyushu, Tokyo, Kanagawa, and Osaka remains, and we have no choice but to rely on steady expansion through branch openings.
