INTRODUCTION

Nippon Gear sells valve actuators, then services them for up to 50 years

Nippon Gear’s best-known product is a valve actuator: an electric motor and gearbox mounted on an industrial valve. The actuator supplies the force that opens, closes or positions the valve when a control system sends a command. If the actuator fails, the plant can lose remote or automatic control of the steam, fuel, chemicals or water passing through that line.

The company also makes jacks, speed reducers and custom gears for equipment that must move accurately under demanding loads and operating conditions. Together, these products generated ¥7.51 billion of revenue and ¥1.69 billion of OP in FY03/26.

Nippon Gear then earns a second stream of revenue from equipment already in the field. Its service segment inspects, diagnoses, repairs and modifies gear devices and other products the company previously sold. In FY03/26, service generated ¥2.37 billion of revenue and ¥771 million of OP.03

The figures show the business plainly: products provide most of the revenue, while maintenance of previously sold equipment contributes almost one-third of company OP at a 32.5% margin. At the August 21 close of ¥1,299, the operating business was valued at 4.69x FY03/27 guided OP after subtracting net cash.

The investment question

Can Nippon Gear sustain a 20% product margin, and how will management use ¥6.77 billion of net cash?

Service already contributes ¥771 million of OP at a 32.5% margin. The share outcome now depends on two concrete decisions: whether the product business keeps its improved profitability as backlog ships, and whether Seiwa-controlled management reinvests or returns the cash instead of continuing to accumulate it.

01 · The equity story

Margins improved before nuclear policy drove the shares to ¥2,200

Earnings started the re-rating. Expectations of U.S. nuclear projects then pushed the shares far beyond the value of any disclosed Nippon Gear order.

Nippon Gear closed at ¥534 on July 31, 2024. The shares remained below ¥500 through much of the following year even as the product business became steadily more profitable. Recognition began during FY03/26. The stock was ¥466 when Nippon Gear released its FY03/25 results on May 13, 2025. By July 31 it had reached ¥629, and it closed at ¥850 when the FY03/26 first-half result was released on October 31.

The next move came from nuclear policy rather than company earnings. At 10:21 a.m. on February 19, Minkabu Press reported that Japan was considering nuclear plants and next-generation reactors for the second U.S.-investment package and wrote that Nippon Gear was bid limit-up.11 The shares closed at ¥1,092 that day, up 15.9%, rose another 22.4% to ¥1,337 on February 20 and reached a peak close of ¥2,200 on March 9.

On March 19, Japan and the United States confirmed up to $40 billion of GE Vernova Hitachi small-modular-reactor projects in Tennessee and Alabama.12 The announcement named GE Vernova Hitachi, not Nippon Gear. The market was pricing Nippon Gear as a possible beneficiary of nuclear construction before the company had disclosed an order from those projects. The shares subsequently fell to a post-peak closing low of ¥1,177 on April 7.

Company earnings remained strong. FY03/26 OP reached ¥2.46 billion, up 16.7% YoY. FY03/27 first-quarter orders rose 12.1% YoY, and backlog reached ¥7.50 billion at June 30.04 At ¥1,299, the market still recognizes the earnings improvement that lifted the shares from ¥534, but it has removed most of the premium attached to the ¥2,200 nuclear-policy peak. The current case therefore rests on product-margin durability and capital allocation, not on an assumed U.S. reactor order.

02 · How the investment works

One actuator can generate scheduled service work for 30 to 50 years

Nippon Gear sells the actuator once, then returns for inspections, diagnostics, parts, overhauls and modifications throughout its operating life.

Nippon Gear says its products normally last 10 to 20 years and can remain in use for 30 to 50 years with continued maintenance.01 That long life creates recurring work because the actuator’s gears, bearings, stem nut, seals, lubricant and electrical components wear at different rates.

The company gives customers a defined inspection cycle. A Level A inspection is performed on site every year. Level B work is performed on site every three to five years and includes removing the motor to inspect gears and electrical parts. Every seven to ten years, a Level C inspection brings the actuator back to Nippon Gear’s factory for a complete overhaul, replacement of worn parts and performance testing.02

Nippon Gear also sells diagnostic services between overhauls. MAC measures torque while a motor-operated valve moves and identifies problems in the drive train or valve. eMAC uses an initial torque measurement as a baseline, then checks motor current from the control center to track deterioration at lower cost. MOVDAS uses a built-in torque sensor and waveform analysis to monitor wear and abnormal operation while the valve remains in service.02

More than 300,000 electric actuators have been produced, and Nippon Gear supports them with service offices, maker parts, repair procedures and factory testing. Each installed unit can therefore produce a sequence of annual checks, periodic detailed inspections, replacement parts, repairs and eventual modifications. FY03/26 service revenue of ¥2.37 billion and OP of ¥771 million show that this installed-base work already contributes materially to earnings.

03 · Decisive question one
01
The operating question

Has the product business reset above a 20% margin?

Service was already profitable. The large change in Nippon Gear’s earnings came from the product segment.

Product OP margin rose from 1.2% in FY03/22 to 11.3% in FY03/23, 17.4% in FY03/24, 17.9% in FY03/25 and 22.4% in FY03/26. Product OP increased by ¥393 million in FY03/26, while service OP declined by ¥43 million. Products therefore supplied the entire increase in company OP.0317

The group income statement shows the same improvement. FY03/26 revenue rose 3.4% YoY while cost of sales fell 4.0% YoY. Gross margin increased 4.0ppt from 44.6% to 48.6%, and OP rose 16.7% YoY. Nippon Gear earned more from each yen of revenue rather than relying on volume alone.

Management’s May 2024 plan set company-wide cumulative targets for FY03/25–FY03/27. If FY03/27 guidance is achieved, the three-year total will reach 98.1% of the revenue target, 117.7% of the OP target and 126.5% of the net-income target.05 The improvement in profit has already run well ahead of the original plan.

FY03/27 first-quarter product OP margin was 22.6%. Valve-actuator orders rose 16.6% YoY, and total backlog increased 31.5% from March to ¥7.50 billion. Backlog now equals 75% of full-year sales guidance.

Headline company OP rose ¥117 million YoY in the quarter. A favorable ¥75 million swing in pension accounting supplied roughly two-thirds of that increase.04 The cleaner operating measure is therefore the product-segment margin itself.

The next result should show whether the ¥7.50 billion backlog can move through production while product OP margin remains around 20%. Holding that level would confirm that the five-year improvement has become the normal earnings base.

04 · Decisive question two
02
The ownership question

Will management return surplus capital or keep accumulating it?

Nippon Gear had ¥6.83 billion of cash and only ¥63 million of interest-bearing debt at June 30, 2026. Net cash was ¥6.77 billion, equal to 36.6% of the company’s ¥18.49 billion market capitalization. The company also held ¥1.77 billion of investment securities.04

Nippon Gear is funding product development, equipment replacement and a new heat-treatment facility. The largest disclosed project is ¥1.48 billion of seismic work at the Fujisawa factory, scheduled for completion after December 2027.08 That project equals about 22% of current net cash and will be paid over several years. Meanwhile, the business generated ¥1.79 billion of FCF in FY03/26 after spending on tangible and intangible assets.

Shareholders received ¥10 per share, an 8.1% payout, and Nippon Gear has no active buyback. The company also held ¥1.77 billion of investment securities, including 19 listed cross-shareholdings with an FY03/26 book value of ¥1.48 billion. Cash and securities are therefore likely to keep growing unless management changes its return policy.

The controlling shareholder is Seiwa Co., Ltd., a Tokyo-based industrial-valve distributor that also sells Nippon Gear actuators, jacks and gears.14 Seiwa holds 39.69% of voting rights. Nippon Gear President Haruo Terada13 is also a Seiwa director, and Nippon Gear sold ¥1.23 billion of products through Seiwa in FY03/26.06

Capital allocation therefore depends on a controlling shareholder that is also a commercial counterparty. A stated cash ceiling, a higher payout, a buyback or a timetable for reducing listed cross-shareholdings would convert surplus capital into a measurable benefit for minority shareholders. If management continues to retain the cash, investors are likely to keep valuing the operating business at a discount.

At ¥1,299, Nippon Gear’s market capitalization is ¥18.49 billion. Subtracting ¥6.77 billion of net cash gives an EV of ¥11.73 billion. That is 4.69x the company’s FY03/27 OP forecast of ¥2.50 billion and 4.77x FY03/26 OP of ¥2.46 billion.

Using FY03/27 revenue guidance, the FY03/26 segment mix and a 32.5% service margin, a 15% product margin produces approximately ¥1.92 billion of group OP. A 20% product margin produces ¥2.30 billion, while the first-quarter product margin of 22.6% reproduces the company’s ¥2.50 billion guidance.

At a 6x operating multiple, the August 21 close sits almost exactly on the ¥1,291 cell: a 22.6% product margin with half of net cash included in equity value. If product margin settles at 20%, the current price requires investors to recognize more than half of the net cash, apply more than 6x to OP, or both. At a 15% product margin, even full recognition of net cash produces ¥1,284 per share. The current price therefore requires either durable product profitability or a capital policy that gives shareholders clearer access to the cash.

06 · Better disclosure

What investors should ask management

Break out recurring service revenue

Report inspections, diagnostics, parts, repair and modification separately, together with the active installed base.

Explain the 22% product margin

Separate price, mix, procurement, productivity and pension effects so investors can judge whether the margin will persist.

Set a cash ceiling and return policy

State how much cash operations require and how dividends, buybacks and cross-holding reductions will use the remainder.
