01 · Rising phase

What moved the share price over the past two years?

One Career operates a service where new graduate job hunters can read other students' experiences for free in exchange for posting their own interview experiences. Two out of every three job hunters sign up, and companies pay a monthly fee to post job openings to this student base and contact candidates directly. The database of screening testimonials is the company's core asset and is difficult to replace with other services.

01 · The rally One Career listed on Mothers in October 2021 and moved to Growth in April 2022, reporting one Career Data Platform segment. Its core asset is a database of selection experiences built since 2015. Students contribute interview questions, interviewers and process duration, and can read others’ accounts free. More than 700,000 entries provide first-hand information on Japanese company recruiting. Companies pay monthly to list jobs and message students. Unique information brings students back; each year’s additions make the database harder to replicate. About two in three new graduates use it, and cumulative corporate customers reached 6,858 at Q1 FY26, up 47%. Revenue comes from listings, scouting, sponsored articles, career events and success-based recruiting, alongside a small career-change service using graduates as future customers. Adding a company costs little because data collection and systems are largely fixed costs. FY25 sales were ¥7.58bn (+40.3%) and OP ¥2.13bn (+64.2%), with margin rising from 6.1% five years earlier to 28.1%. Domestic small-cap AI interest and March 16, 2025’s three-for-one split supported the shares, which reached ¥2,892 on August 26.

02 · The reversal August’s high coincided with the first acquisition announcement. On August 21, 2025, One Career announced additional purchases of Lightrose, operator of CAMPUS REACH, raising ownership from 17.8% to 100%; the shares reached ¥2,892 five days later. Alongside strong growth, investors priced in possible AI use of the experience database and an AI-software valuation. Buying weakened in autumn. Monthly business-environment FAQs began then, showing recognition of the need to explain the business to individual and overseas investors between results releases. November 14’s Q3 results brought the first annual dividend increase, from ¥14 to ¥21, and a payout target increase from 20% to 30%. The shares still drifted down as the summer premium across domestic small-cap AI faded. OP grew sharply in February 12, 2026 FY25 results, by which time the price was well below the high.

03 · Current position February, March and May 2026 disclosures changed the focus. February 12’s first consolidated full-year results showed ¥7.58bn sales (+40.3%), ¥2.13bn OP (+64.2%) and ¥1.5bn net profit (+62.5%). FY26 guidance was ¥10.5bn sales (+38.6%), ¥3bn OP (+41.0%) and ¥34 DPS, consistent with the roughly 30% payout target first stated three months earlier. February 24’s new executive structure promoted CFO Tomoaki Kimura to director and executive officer to accelerate M&A. On March 26, management announced acquisitions of Zero One Brain and Kids Corporation. Kids would become an indirect subsidiary at about 5x adjusted EBITDA, with a network of roughly 5,000 schools, ¥2.6bn standalone sales and ¥0.28bn OP; consolidation begins in Q3 FY26. May 14’s first consolidated Q1 showed ¥2.21bn sales (+47.2%), ¥0.66bn OP (+100.8%) and a 30.1% margin, achieving 21.0% of sales guidance and 22.1% of OP guidance. The shares closed at ¥1,947 on Monday, May 25, 33% below August 2025’s high and 106% above August 2024’s low. Watch whether OP exceeds guidance, whether Kids adds profit or lowers consolidated margins, and whether management starts disclosing profitability by business and acquisition.

02 · Issue

Three questions dividing investor views

Based on

's most recent quarterly financial results and IR briefing materials, we will summarize three issues that should be confirmed in the future.

Issue 01 · Progress in the first quarter
Q1 In FY26, sales reached 21.0% of the full-year forecast and operating profit of 22.1%. Is this a sign that the full-year forecast will be exceeded, or is it just a seasonal factor?
BULL Bulls believe that Q1 operating profit margin30.1%It is noted that the cost of sales ratio also decreased from 13.4% in the same period of the previous year to 11.1%. For the new graduate platform, expenses do not increase easily even when sales increase, and we believe that profitability has improved due to scale expansion rather than a temporary decrease in expenses.This view will hold true if the 1H operating profit margin reaches 28% or more at the end of Q2 FY26 and the full-year operating profit progresses at a pace higher than ¥3,200M.In the end, it exceeded the company's forecast by about 7%, confirming that the decline in the expense ratio was not due to a lag in the timing of recording, but rather a continuous improvement.
BEAR Bearish critics point out that monthly sales may be biased toward Q1 due to the timing of new graduate recruitment, and that quarterly sales for each product are not disclosed. The low cost of sales ratio in Q1 may be due to post-acquisition integration costs being recorded in a later quarter.This view will hold true if the 1H operating profit margin at the end of Q2 FY26 is within ±1.0pt of ~28.6% calculated backwards from the company's FY26 forecast, and the upward revision to the full-year forecast remains below 3%.
Issue 02 · Kids Corporation
Will Kids Corporation's sales network of 5,000 schools be the foundation for growth over the next 10 years? Or will the consolidated profit margin be lowered and the original growth of the new graduate business obscured?
BULL Bulls believe that by using Kids Corporation's network, the company can enter the high school recruitment market earlier than its competitors. Through our sales network of 5,000 schools, we can directly sell to companies that single carriers could not do business with before.Q4 If Kids Corporation's sales are disclosed separately in the FY26 or FY27 1H financial results briefing materials, and the year-on-year growth rate is accelerating, this view would hold true.Within 12 months of the start of consolidation, companies that newly use OneCareer's new graduate platformMore than 50 companiesThis fact also serves as a basis for confirming the results of mutual sales.
BEAR Bearish critics point out that Kids Corporation's standalone operating profit margin is around 11%, well below the more than 30% for new graduate platforms. Even if the integration goes smoothly and the company is able to maintain its own profit, the consolidated operating profit margin will decline for at least 18 months.Kids Corporation's contribution to FY27 operating profitLess than ¥150M(¥279M for the fiscal year ending March 2025 on a non-consolidated basis) or the FY27 consolidated operating profit marginbelow 27%(28.1% in FY25), this view holds true.
Issue 03 · Positioning of AI
Will the database of selection experience stories continue to be the first place students visit even in the age of AI?
BULL Bullists believe that it will be difficult for an external AI service to reproduce the more than 700,000 selection experiences confirmed by students themselves in a short period of time. Gathering the same information would require years of building a student base of similar size. One Career itself has begun using AI for both internal operations and member-facing functions.This view can be verified once AI-related usage indicators are disclosed for the first time in the FY26 securities report (expected to be submitted around March 2027).The cumulative number of people who use AI functions every day (approx. 2,420,000 people)30% or moreIf so, we can conclude that our company is now the gateway to using AI, rather than losing users to external AI.
BEAR Those who are bearish believe that the very way students search for places to apply will change. When students who are about to graduate start asking external AI services about where to apply, their first stop will be AI, not just one career. Even if unique testimonials retain business customers, it may take time for companies to realize that student visits are declining. The usage rate of the new graduate platform was 64.5% among members graduating in 2026 (FY26 Q1 financial results briefing material).Even at the time of FY27 1H financial results briefing materials, the usage index of AI functions was not disclosed, and this usage rate was200bps or moreIf it declines, this view holds true.

One carrier is disclosed as a single segment. Since Lightrose will be consolidated from Q1 FY26 and Kids Corporation from Q3 FY26, the unit economics of all three groups will be consolidated into one sales line. Outside investors cannot differentiate and evaluate the new graduate franchise, which has an operating profit margin of over 30%, the smaller mid-career business, and the high school network advertising business, which has an operating profit margin of 11%, based on published numbers alone. Adding four items to each quarter's supplementary slide will allow the market to evaluate franchises and acquisitions separately. Specifically(i) Sales of new graduates and mid-career employees, (ii) Product composition of job postings, scouts, personnel introductions, events, and tie-up articles, (iii) Contribution of acquired companies by segment, (iv) Ratio of continuing sales to consolidated totalIt is. There are four items of additional information required. The multiplier effect affects the discount that the market is currently pricing in for uncertainty, not the profits themselves.

The company has clearly stated disciplined standards regarding M&A. i.e.EV/EBITDA less than 5 times, goodwill ratio to net assets less than 50%, size of one deal less than 15% of market capitalizationThere are three conditions. On the other hand, performance trends in line with these standards are not disclosed. FY25 net profit is¥1.5bn, dividends absorbed about 30%, leaving roughly ¥1.04bn of retained earnings for reinvestment. Capex used ¥278M and Lightrose’s acquisition used ¥32M of cash, within the ¥119M investment cash flow remaining after capex. Net cash still rose ¥1.81bn YoY. Trailing ROE is 31%, and Greenblatt ROIC, OP divided by working capital plus fixed assets, is 38%. A 5x EV/EBITDA acquisition ceiling implies roughly a 20% marginal yield per deal, far above the 0.4% cash yield replaced but below the core business’s internal return. Three acquisitions were announced in nine months, with Kids Corporation closing in July. Cash retained at 38% ROCE must compound at an unproven acquisition return rather than the established franchise’s own return.Unless the return on invested capital for each acquisition target is disclosed, the market cannot judge whether each M&A is increasing the corporate value of the core business or diluting profitability. Disclosure that can be confirmed on a case-by-case and quarterly basis is necessary.
