INTRODUCTION

What does Yappli do?

Yappli provides a no-code mobile-app platform. Companies can build and operate iOS and Android apps without writing most code in-house. The paying customer is the enterprise or operator that runs the app; end users are its shoppers, members, or employees. The product value is ongoing operation — content updates, push notifications, ID and point-card functions, and app-store compatibility support — not just initial launch. Roughly 80% of revenue comes from recurring platform subscriptions, with the rest from implementation and growth support. Once app operations sit inside Yappli’s workflow, switching frictions can rise. The business becomes more valuable if customer growth and higher subscription revenue outweigh the cost of winning and serving each account. Churn, monthly subscription fees per logo, and contract growth will show whether that is happening.

01 · THE RALLY Yappli sells a no-code mobile-app platform used by non-developers at retail-apparel chains, restaurant operators, sports teams, and a growing internal-app customer set. In practice, customers are not only buying app creation. They are buying daily app operations inside one system: Apple and Google store-review handling, OS-update compatibility, push notifications, member ID, point-card functions, and content management. Revenue comes in two layers: recurring platform subscriptions at around 80% of sales, plus one-time professional-services support (creation support at signing and growth support after launch) at around 20%.

02 · THE REVERSAL On November 13, 2025 management did two things in a single disclosure. First, it upward-revised the FY12/25 OP guide from ¥750M to ¥830M and raised the dividend forecast in tandem; read alone, the revision should have extended the climb. Second, a separate release the same day announced the acquisition of Choose Monster for ¥130M cash, of which ¥113M — 87% of the purchase price — was recognized as goodwill amortized over nine years, with no published comparison to the alternative of repurchasing ¥130M of Yappli stock at the then-prevailing valuation.

03 · WHERE WE STAND NOW The 1Q FY12/26 print landed on May 13, 2026: revenue ¥1,707M (+19.1% YoY), OP ¥396M (+77.6% YoY), net income ¥390M (+75.1% YoY), and OP margin at an all-time high of 23.2% — 8.5ppt above the FY12/26 full-year guide of 14.7%. Gross-revenue churn (LTM) improved from 0.92% to 0.86%, ahead of management's prior "2H FY12/26 improvement" timeline. The lifetime-value-to-acquisition-cost ratio climbed to 6.6x, above management's own 5x re-investment threshold.

02 · CONTENTION

Which debates are driving the stock now?

Retention, margins, and capital allocation now drive the debate.

DEBATE 01 · MARGIN DURABILITY
Is the 1Q margin durable, or will resumed advertising reverse it?
BULL
  • The first-quarter result shows operating leverage despite a higher cost base. If advertising returns to the planned pace, the durability of that margin will depend on subscription growth rather than timing.
BEAR
  • The 1Q 23.2% margin embeds professional-services mix favorability (creation-support pulled forward) plus an ad-spend timing benefit of roughly ¥30M as 1Q ran below the ¥180M / quarter guided cadence.
DEBATE 02 · CAPITAL RETURN
Is the ¥15 dividend temporary, or the natural ceiling while Yappli invests?
BULL
  • Yappli now runs a 37% ROE with ¥1.04bn of net cash.
BEAR
  • Management still puts growth investment first. The November 2025 Choose Monster acquisition showed that priority: ¥130M was deployed with 87% recorded as goodwill, and management has published no post-mortem.
DEBATE 03 · MULTI-PRODUCT PIVOT
Does the four-product line lift average revenue per logo and total contract count, or dilute the headline subscription economics?
BULL
  • Average monthly subscription fee per logo held steady at ¥466k (+3.9% YoY) despite WebX and MiniApp being lower-priced.
  • Cited cross-sell wins at Alpen, Dr. Martens, and Sarutahiko Coffee show enterprises consolidating more of their digital operations on Yappli.
BEAR
  • Cohort-level retention is not disclosed; HR / UNITE growth may be diluting an elevated Marketing-cohort number.

The bear band of ¥550–¥650 implies ~4.6–5.6x FY12/26 forward EV/OP — meaningfully below the Japanese mid-cap SaaS peer band of 10–15x and below the JP private-buyer 7–10x cohort for asset-light specialty technology businesses.
