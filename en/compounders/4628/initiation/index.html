INTRODUCTION

SK KAKEN (4628)

A major apartment-building renovation involves far more than paint. Scaffolding has to be erected, surfaces repaired and prepared, trades coordinated, and residents accommodated while the work is carried out. For the building owner, the relevant question is not how many kilograms of coating are used, but how long the building will remain protected before the next large renovation is required.

SK KAKEN operates squarely in that market. It makes architectural finishing materials used to protect and renew building exteriors and says it holds approximately 52% of Japan’s architectural finishing-material market. Its products reach projects through distributors and contractors, but the company also works upstream with developers, general contractors, architects and building owners, seeking to have its products incorporated into specifications before the final order is placed.0303

That position matters because customers are buying performance rather than coating by weight. Durability, appearance, ease of application and the interval until the next renovation all affect the economics of a project. A supplier that helps shape the specification can therefore capture more value from each renovation even if the market consumes less material overall.

Renovation spending is rising as coating volume falls

In CY2025, architectural finishing-material production fell 8.5% YoY to 144,370 tonnes, while the largest renovation-coating category fell 9.8% to 47,060 tonnes. Over roughly the same period, MLIT reported that building-renovation and renewal orders for the year ended March 2026 rose 18.7% to ¥16.4104tn.0102

Those two trends can coexist quite naturally. Coating is only one part of a renovation budget. Scaffolding, labor, surface preparation, project management and disruption to occupants all carry costs. As those costs rise, a coating that extends the life of the renovation becomes more valuable. Less material can be used while the economic value of each project increases.

SK KAKEN already sells into that logic. Products such as Premium Silicone are marketed on high durability and lower lifecycle costs. Combined with the company’s upstream role in specification, that creates a plausible route to higher revenue per renovation even as industry tonnage declines.05

The financial record is consistent with that possibility. Group sales rose from ¥88.282bn in FY03/22 to ¥109.707bn in FY03/26. FY03/26 OP was ¥12.218bn, CFO was ¥12.758bn and cash-defined FCF was ¥9.196bn.04

Price, however, has also been a major driver. SK KAKEN had already revised prices in 2023, a fact referenced again in its April 7, 2026 announcement. The 2026 increases were substantial: 15–25% for water-based products, 20–30% for solvent-based products and 10–15% for powder products, reflecting higher raw-material costs and supply constraints.060706

That makes it impossible to attribute the divergence between rising SK KAKEN revenue and falling industry tonnage solely to premium mix, stronger specification power or market-share gains. Part of the increase is clearly pricing. SK KAKEN does not disclose the unit, price, mix and share bridge needed to separate those effects.

The more interesting possibility is therefore still unproven. Better durability can reduce repaint frequency and material consumption, so SK KAKEN does not need industry tonnes to grow. What matters is whether it can continue capturing more economic value from each renovation after the latest price reset.

02 · Decisive question one
01
The operating question

FY03/27 1Q was distorted by pulled-forward demand

FY03/27 1Q appeared exceptionally strong. Sales rose 21.2% YoY to ¥31.753bn, OP rose 84.4% to ¥5.198bn, and the operating margin reached 16.4%.08

The company’s explanation makes clear that this was not a normal run rate. Customers accelerated orders ahead of expected solvent shortages and feared future price increases, and SK KAKEN said it expected a reversal afterward. Despite the strong quarter, H1 guidance remained ¥54.1bn of sales and ¥5.4bn of OP, while full-year guidance stayed at ¥112bn of sales and ¥11.4bn of OP.08

The implied Q2 numbers show how much demand moved forward. The unchanged H1 forecast leaves Q2 sales of ¥22.347bn and OP of just ¥0.202bn, equivalent to an operating margin of about 0.9%.

In FY03/26, H1 sales were ¥52.963bn and OP was ¥5.764bn. Subtracting the prior Q1 gives prior-year Q2 sales of ¥26.755bn, OP of ¥2.944bn and an 11.0% margin.0809

The 16.4% margin reported in FY03/27 1Q should therefore not be treated as a new base. The supply constraints and large price increases announced in April also fit the company’s explanation that orders were brought forward.06

A sensible normalized range for group OP remains ¥10–12bn, with the company’s ¥11.4bn full-year guidance sitting inside that range. Once the pull-forward has washed through, the more informative question will be whether SK KAKEN can sustain ordinary sales and margins while industry coating volumes continue to decline.

03 · Decisive question two
02
The capital-allocation question

Financial assets exceed the market capitalization

SK KAKEN’s balance sheet changes the character of the investment.

At March 31, 2026, the company held ¥105.240bn of cash and deposits, ¥26.011bn of long-term deposits and ¥18.956bn of investment securities against ¥3.000bn of debt. On that basis, broad financial assets net of debt at book value were ¥147.207bn.10

That is not the same as immediately available cash. The investment securities are mainly held-to-maturity bonds with maturities extending one to five years, while the long-term deposits include option-bearing or extendible deposits. Using disclosed fair values reduces broad financial assets net of debt slightly, to about ¥146.647bn.10

Even after that adjustment, the scale is striking. SK KAKEN’s market capitalization at the August 25, 2026 close was approximately ¥122.760bn, below the size of the broader financial-asset pool.

The operating business continues to add to that balance sheet. FY03/26 CFO was ¥12.758bn and cash acquisitions of fixed assets were ¥3.562bn, leaving cash-defined FCF of ¥9.196bn. A mature business producing FCF at that level will continue accumulating financial assets unless the cash is reinvested or returned.04

The unanswered issue is how much of that value will ultimately reach each share.

In 2024, AVI Japan Opportunity Trust proposed cancelling 1,973,183 treasury shares and raising the FY03/24 total dividend to ¥290 per share. SK KAKEN’s board opposed both proposals, emphasizing the value of retaining financial flexibility for investment and other corporate needs.11

The company now refers to ROE above 8% and P/B above 1.0x as targets, but it has not set out a quantified capital-allocation framework linking those goals to the accumulated financial assets. FY03/26’s ¥230 dividend included a ¥50 anniversary dividend; the ordinary dividend was ¥180, and FY03/27 guidance also calls for ¥180.1004

Treasury-share cancellation is a separate capital-structure issue. It does not distribute cash to shareholders, and cancelling shares already excluded from shares outstanding does not reduce the number of shares in circulation.

The ¥147.207bn financial-asset pool is therefore genuine balance-sheet value, but its contribution to per-share value depends on how much capital the business needs and how the remainder is ultimately used.

04 · Valuation

What the current price implies

SK KAKEN closed at ¥9,100 on August 25, 2026. With 13.4901m shares excluding treasury, its market capitalization was approximately ¥122.760bn.08

Cash equivalents of ¥45.748bn less ¥3.000bn of debt give conventional net cash of ¥42.748bn. Subtracting that from market capitalization gives conventional EV of about ¥80.012bn, equivalent to about 7.0x FY03/27 guided OP/EBIT of ¥11.4bn.

At ¥9,100, the shares do not require aggressive earnings growth to support the current valuation. Using only conventional net cash, the price already sits within the range implied by normalized OP of ¥10–12bn.

The broader balance sheet makes the valuation look more unusual. Mechanically subtracting the ¥147.207bn of broad financial assets net of debt at book value from the ¥122.760bn market capitalization produces a broad-asset-adjusted EV of about negative ¥24.447bn. That is around -2.0x normalized EBIT/OP of about ¥12bn, or about -2.2x FY03/27 guided OP/EBIT of ¥11.4bn.

The broad-asset-adjusted EV is not a realizable valuation or a price target. Not all of the financial assets are immediately liquid, not all can be assumed to be excess to the needs of the business, and no commitment has been made to return them to shareholders. The calculation simply shows how large the financial-asset base is relative to the market value of the company.

The conventional valuation is already inexpensive. The much larger financial-asset pool sits on top of that as a potential source of per-share value, with realization dependent on future capital allocation.

05 · Future disclosure

Investment conclusion

SK KAKEN operates in a market where renovation spending is rising even as coating volumes decline. Its approximately 52% domestic share and ability to influence specifications before the final order is placed give it a credible opportunity to capture more value from each renovation.

Large price increases mean that the recent revenue resilience cannot yet be separated cleanly into pricing, premium mix, specification strength and market-share gains. FY03/27 1Q was also distorted by pulled-forward demand, leaving ¥10–12bn of normalized group OP as the more sensible earnings base.

Even on that restrained view, the operating business trades at only about 7.0x guided EV/EBIT and does not require heroic earnings growth to support the current price. The balance sheet adds a second source of potential value, but the financial assets should remain an option rather than assumed per-share value until capital allocation provides a clearer route to realization.

If SK KAKEN can show that it is genuinely capturing more value per renovation after the price reset, while putting its accumulated capital to more productive use, the current valuation leaves meaningful room for reassessment.
