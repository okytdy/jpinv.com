Where JII is called in

Three situations that bring companies to us

Start with when JII gets the call. If any one of these is familiar, the rest of this page will be useful.

Situation 01

Results are days away. The Japanese tanshin is ready. The English will not be.

Every disclosure needs a translation on a short deadline. When the work moves between vendors, the same term comes back in different English each time, and the reader takes it as a different subject.

Situation 02

An overseas fund has asked for a meeting, and it will be held in English.

An interpreter is easy to book. One who understands both the business and the accounting is not. Questions get answered slightly beside the point, and the meeting ends without either side noticing.

Situation 03

The English materials go out. Whether anyone reads them is unknown.

Where an overseas investor stops and rereads is invisible from inside the company. Silence and comprehension look identical from the outside.

How the three connect

Disclosure leads to a meeting. The meeting shows what to improve.

The three look like separate jobs. They run in sequence. An investor reads the English disclosure, finds something worth asking about, and requests a meeting. The questions asked in that meeting are a direct report on what the document failed to convey. Feed that back into the next disclosure, and the next meeting starts further along.

JII takes all three because the sequence has to close inside one firm. Hire a translation vendor and an interpretation vendor separately, and what surfaces in the meeting never travels back to the document. Here the same person is present at both ends, so whatever stalled the room gets fixed in the next translation.

Each circle links through to that service.
