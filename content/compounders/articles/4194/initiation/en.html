01 · Stock price trends

What moved the share price over the past two years?

During the same period, TOPIX rose by by about +42%, while Visional's was -7%. This is a decline that is difficult to explain solely by movements in the market as a whole or in human resources-related stocks. We will clarify what investors are reevaluating and where there are still differences in their views.

A · April–August 2025 · +67% The rally reflected a changing assessment of the business rather than one earnings release. June 2025 Q3 FY7/25 results showed BizReach growing 18.8% YoY with a 43.7% OP margin before allocations, while HRMOS ARR reached ¥3.49bn (+32.8%) and losses continued as planned during investment. Strong profit in the existing business and expansion of the next growth engine encouraged investors to see multiple earnings sources rather than a single recruiting service. August’s ¥12,310 high represented about 14x next-twelve-month EV/EBIT, above Visional’s established valuation range.

B · August 2025–February 2026 · −48% The decline did not follow a major earnings miss. September’s FY7/25 results were solid and FY7/26 guidance envisaged sales and profit growth. The issue was the visible investment burden: internal BizReach, Thinkings/sonar ATS integration and Incubation reinvestment were brought forward, leaving OP growing less than sales. December’s Q1 disclosure showed margin pressure when investors had expected expansion, and the multiple fell further. Investors could not distinguish HRMOS’s underlying improvement from sonar ATS consolidation. Rate-rise expectations also made domestic employers more cautious about hiring, renewing attention to BizReach’s cyclical exposure.

Company factors or market factors? The decline cannot be explained by the wider market alone. Over the same 24 months, Visional fell 7% while TOPIX rose 42%, a gap of about 49ppt. That is too large to attribute solely to the cycle or weakness in recruitment stocks. Investors reassessed what they would pay when the relationship between growth investment and capital efficiency remained unclear.

C · March 2026 · +16% from the low March 17’s Q2 FY7/26 results eased worst-case concerns. H1 sales were ¥46.6bn (+26.2%), the HR business OP margin was 33%, and HRMOS was profitable both in H1 and Q2. HRMOS series ARR was ¥8.96bn, up 181% under an expanded definition including sonar ATS; full-year guidance was unchanged. The shares recovered 15% from ¥6,347, but the multiple had not recovered. The gap between the recent ¥7,309 price and August 2025’s high was more about disclosure than earnings. Until investors can assess HRMOS’s underlying profitability separately from M&A consolidation, EV/EBIT around 8x is unlikely to return to about 14x.
