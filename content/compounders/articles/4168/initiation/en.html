01 · Share-price regime

What moved the share price over the past two years?

stock price rose +116% in 4 months from ¥585 on April 7, 2025 to ¥1,263 on August 18 of the same year. The move reflected expectations for growth through the initial dividend and multiple products. It then fell about 40% over nine months. The turning point was the acquisition of Chew's Monster (currently Yappli Food Connect Co., Ltd.) announced on November 13, 2025. It was disclosed on the same day as the upward revision of FY25 company forecasts, and ¥113 million yen (87%) of the acquisition consideration ¥130 million yen was recorded as goodwill. In the final financial results on February 13, 2026, the company explained that the tax savings from loss carryforwards will be used up in FY26. The return of net profit to a level that reflects normal tax burden from FY27 has also changed the outlook on stock prices.

01 · The rally Yappli’s main business is no-code SaaS that lets customers build iOS and Android apps without specialist engineers and operate them after launch. Customers span apparel, restaurants, sports teams and media, with adoption for internal employee communication also growing in HR. Its strength lies less in app-building features than in continuously handling app-store reviews, OS updates, push notifications, member IDs, points and CMS. LTM churn in Q1 FY26 was 0.86%, down from 0.92%. Recurring fees account for about 80% of sales and implementation services about 20%. Since 2024, Yappli has expanded to four products by adding WebX, MiniApp and MobileOrder. In Q1 FY26, contracts totaled 958, GPM was 69.8%, monthly ARPU ¥466,000 and LTV/CAC 6.6x. Implementation support opens the relationship, while additional products connect to existing CRM, ID and points infrastructure to increase business per customer. The shares reached a 24-month low of ¥585 on April 7, 2025 and began recovering on May 14, when the Q1 FY25 results were accompanied by a dividend policy change and a first annual dividend of ¥12. This was raised to ¥13 on November 13, giving an FY25 payout of 18.1%. Beginning shareholder returns alongside growth investment changed the market’s view. The August 13, 2025 Q2 results showed growth in implementation services and progress with Yappli WebX, launched in May 2025 using smartLP software assets acquired in October 2024. The price reached ¥1,263 on August 18, up 116% in four months. Based on guidance at the time, that represented about 15x next-twelve-month EV/EBIT, a high valuation even for a profitable domestic mid-cap SaaS company.

02 · Adjustment from the highs On November 13, 2025, management made two announcements. It raised FY25 OP guidance from ¥750M to ¥830M and increased the dividend forecast, an upgrade that in isolation could have sustained the rally. Separately, it announced the ¥130M cash acquisition of Choose Monster, now Yappli Food Connect. Of this, ¥113M (87%) was recognized as goodwill to be amortized straight-line over nine years. No framework was provided for subsequently comparing the deal’s IRR with a buyback of the same amount. As investors reassessed execution risk in the multi-product strategy through autumn, the shares fell from the ¥900s to the ¥700s. FY25 results on February 13, 2026 showed sales of ¥6,056M, parent-only OP of ¥889M (+61.5%) and net profit of ¥920M, exceeding the revised November guidance. However, FY26 guidance of ¥1,000M OP (+13.3%) and ¥930M net profit (+1.0%) implied slower growth. At the briefing, management said tax benefits from loss carryforwards would be exhausted in FY26 and FY27 net profit would normalize toward underlying operating earnings. This provided another catalyst for multiple compression, with the price moving from the ¥800s to the ¥700s through winter and spring. The reversal combines two effects: a reasonable adjustment to slower growth from FY25, and an excessive reaction to the FY27 expiry of tax benefits.

03 · Current position Q1 FY26 results were released on May 13, 2026. Sales were ¥1,707M (+19.1%), OP ¥396M (+77.6%) and net profit ¥390M (+75.1%). The 23.2% OP margin exceeded full-year guidance of 14.7% by 8.5ppt and was the highest since listing. LTM churn improved from 0.92% to 0.86%, one to two quarters ahead of the improvement management had expected in H2 FY26. LTV/CAC of 6.6x exceeded its 5x reinvestment threshold, and contracts across four products totaled 958. Cross-selling examples included Alpen, Dr. Martens and Sarutahiko Coffee. On the same day, management raised the FY26 annual dividend for a second time, from ¥14 to ¥15, implying a 20.6% payout. Four executive officers had already been appointed: COO, CFO and CHRO effective April 1, and CoS effective January 1. The CFO appointment in particular suggests that capital allocation discipline is becoming part of the management structure. The share price recovered to ¥745, but remained 41% below its August 2025 level, leaving about 6.5 turns of forward EV/EBIT compression. Roughly half can be read as a reasonable reservation about whether the Q1 margin is sustainable, and half as an excessive allowance for the FY27 expiry of tax benefits. Three questions should be resolved over the next four quarters: whether the 23.2% Q1 margin reflects a structural improvement, with both Q2 and Q3 FY26 delivering OP margins of at least 18% after advertising normalizes to ¥180M per quarter; whether capital returns become a formal policy with a payout of at least 30% or an explicit total-return formula; and whether churn disclosure by business shows the Marketing cohort remaining below 1.0% on an LTM basis.

Yappli's customer base is organized into three customer areas (cohorts): apps for retail, e-commerce, and apparel (Yappli for Marketing), apps for HR and employee engagement (UNITE by Yappli), and the remaining areas for BtoB, media, and local governments. These three are the company's customer cohorts, and each cohort's retention dynamics determine how much revenue is ultimately collected from a single contract. The longer a contract lasts, the greater the cumulative revenue from that contract.The — company calls this Lifetime Value (LTV). Currently, only one LTM company-wide churn rate is disclosed. The company explained in its Q4 FY25 IR briefing that the increase in headline LTM churn rate from 0.77% to 0.92% was primarily due to the Marketing cohort. Q1 FY260.86%However, it remains unclear from the outside whether the Marketing cohort itself has recovered, or whether the rapid growth in the HR/UNITE field is merely a result of diluting Marketing's still high churn rate.
