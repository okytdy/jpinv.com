01 · Share-price regime

What moved the share price over the past two years?

stock price rose 114% in 4 months from ¥1,170 on April 7, 2025 to ¥2,510 on August 18 of the same year. This move was based on the expectation that Talent Palette would expand to large companies and that profits would increase more than sales growth. Thereafter, the stock price did not fall significantly, and even after three quarters of financial results, ¥1.15bn of goodwill impairment, the company's growth forecast slowing, and the entry of activist investors, the stock price remained within the range of ¥2,000 to ¥2,500. In May 2026, the company revised its shareholder return policy.

01 · The rally Plus Alpha Consulting analyzes Japanese business data, including employee surveys, customer feedback and call-center histories, and presents it in a form managers can use without specialist knowledge. Talent Palette has gained adoption among major listed companies. HR solutions, including Talent Palette, Yorisol, R-Shift and R-Kintai, had 2,171 contracts at the end of Q1 FY9/26. Companies with more than 1,000 employees accounted for 40% of contracts and 73% of Talent Palette MRR. Its proprietary Waters language engine has handled Japanese named entities for 17 years, while internal consultants turn customer requests into product features. Management calls this feedback, product development and resale process the PAC cycle. HR solutions maintained a three-year sales CAGR above 30% while margins increased. Existing-contract pricing and mix lifted Q1 FY9/26 Talent Palette ARPU to ¥468,000, up 11.3%, helped by revisions begun in Q3 FY24 and completed in April 2025, options and upgrades. Targeting large customers cut Q1 SG&A 15.9% YoY and marketing costs about 34%. Channel expansion began with Mynavi TalentBase on October 1, 2025. The shares reached a 24-month low of ¥1,170 on April 7, amid growth-stock weakness and limited Q1 contributions from acquired Grow Up and Attack. H1 FY9/25 OP growth of 28.8% supported recovery; on August 13 management raised FY9/25 OP guidance from ¥5,600M to ¥6,100M. The price reached ¥2,510 on August 18, about 12.3x next-twelve-month EV/EBIT based on guidance, returning to the highest valuation among JII’s covered names within four months.

02 · Adjustment from the highs On November 12, 2025, one release carried three messages. FY9/25 OP guidance rose again from ¥6,100M to ¥6,378M as marketing costs continued falling. Impairments totaled ¥1,154M, including ¥1,092M for Grow Up, acquired in 2022 and operating Kimisuka new-graduate recruiting, and ¥61M for Attack recruitment BPO, acquired in 2024. Both had underperformed acquisition plans, and all remaining goodwill was written off. Management also authorized cancellation on November 28 of all 472,250 treasury shares, or 1.10% before cancellation. November 14 results showed sales of ¥17,084M (+22.8%), OP of ¥6,378M (+40.8%), a reported consolidated OP margin of 37.3% and segment-weighted margin of 44.7%. FY9/26 guidance was ¥19,500M sales (+14.1%), ¥7,500M OP (+17.6%) and a 38.5% margin. The shares barely reacted the next trading day despite slower OP growth: investors treated impairments as a one-off cleanup of acquisitions, alongside cancellation and a dividend increase from ¥29 to ¥38. February 13, 2026 Q1 results confirmed that H2’s margin improvement carried into the new year, with sales of ¥4,439M (+14.0%), OP of ¥1,676M (+49.5%) and margin of 37.8%, versus 28.8% a year earlier.

03 · Current position Two developments changed the discussion in late April and early May 2026. Oasis disclosed a stake rising from 8.02% on April 23 to 10.52% on April 27 and 10.62% on May 1, seeking greater returns, board effectiveness and capital allocation engagement. Its filings envisaged continued proposals over twelve months and acquisition of another 5%. The June 24 amendment showed 16.29%, making Oasis the largest shareholder. H1 results on May 13 showed sales of ¥9,341M (+14.2%), OP of ¥3,692M (+32.2%) and net profit of ¥2,518M (+36.0%). Management raised its payout target from 30% to 40%, following August 2025’s increase from 20% to 30%, and introduced an 8% DOE floor. FY9/26 DPS guidance rose from ¥38 to ¥50, 72% above the prior ¥29. H1 delivered 49.2% of full-year OP guidance and a 39.5% margin, 1ppt above the 38.5% target. Activist-backed returns have partly taken hold, but a 40% payout against ¥5,200M guided net profit implies roughly ¥2,120M (¥50 times 42,390,000 shares), only about 2.1% of market value, alongside ¥14.56bn net cash equal to 14.4% of market value. Watch whether H2 margins hold at least 38%, suggesting scope for an upgrade; whether Mynavi TalentBase OEM gains a separate revenue disclosure demonstrating the partner channel; and whether November’s results pair the 40% payout with recurring buybacks.

Issue 02 · Dependence on talent palette

With an OEM partnership, you can expand your sales network without issuing new shares. In November 2025, the company plans to enter into a capital and business alliance with Lux, and to provide OEM products "Rakuraku Personnel and Labor Affairs" for companies with 300 or fewer employees by FY9/26.
